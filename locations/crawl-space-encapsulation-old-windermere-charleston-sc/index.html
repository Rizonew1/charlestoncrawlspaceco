Crawl space encapsulation in Old Windermere

Old Windermere is a mature West Ashley neighborhood of mid-century homes on tree-shaded lots, a short drive from the Ashley River and the Citadel Mall area. The streets are quiet, the lots are generous and the canopy is thick. It is the kind of neighborhood where people stay for decades.

Long ownership has its own effect on crawl spaces. Houses that were built in the 1950s and 1960s have had decades to collect moisture, and many have been remodeled without anyone addressing what was underneath. We help Old Windermere homeowners find the cause, clear the damage and put in a system that protects the house for the long term.

What makes Old Windermere crawl spaces different

Mature tree cover. Large live oaks, pines and other trees shade the ground and drop debris, leaving soil under houses cool, wet and covered in organic matter.

Mid-century construction. Homes were built when vented crawl spaces and fiberglass batts were standard, before modern ideas about humid climates.

Flat lots. Lots here are level, and water moves slowly across them. Rain can sit against foundations instead of draining away.

Long ownership. Many houses have been in the same family for decades, which means original equipment and original problems.

Remodeled interiors. Many homes have new kitchens, baths and floors, with crawl spaces that were never part of the plan.

Homes in Old Windermere

Typical homes are single-story ranches and split-levels, many of brick or brick veneer, built in the 1950s and 1960s. Larger lots and generous floor plans are common. Some homes have crawl spaces under the entire footprint, while others combine crawl space with a slab section for a garage or den.

Foundations are generally masonry perimeter walls with interior piers, with vents spaced around the outside. Floor systems are wood joists and beams. Ductwork was added or replaced over the years and runs through the crawl space, often with deteriorating insulation and uneven sealing.

Because houses here are larger than the cottages in some nearby neighborhoods, crawl spaces can be more extensive, with more area to clean, seal and condition. They are usually easier to access, and the spaces tend to have more clearance.

Water, soil and flood conditions in Old Windermere

Shaded, slow-drying soil. Tree cover limits sun on the ground, and soil under houses can stay damp for months.

Flat grading. Water spreads out rather than flowing away, so after heavy rain it can linger near foundations.

Roots and debris. Roots can crack foundations and piers. Leaves and branches accumulate around vents and under houses.

Humidity from vents. In summer, humid air enters through vents and condenses on cooler surfaces.

Proximity to the Ashley River. Parts of West Ashley are low and affected by tides and storm surge. We look at conditions house by house.

Crawl space problems we see in Old Windermere

  • Fiberglass insulation, decades old, sagging, wet or moldy
  • Mold on joists and subfloor, particularly near vents
  • Dirt floors or old, torn vapor barriers
  • Roots and organic debris in and around the crawl space
  • Rusted ductwork with failing insulation and loose seams
  • Termites and other pests in damp, shaded areas
  • Rot at sills and rim joists
  • Moisture staining around plumbing
  • Rooms above that feel damp, musty or cold

Larger homes mean larger systems

A ranch with an extensive crawl space is not simply a bigger version of a small cottage. It has more volume to condition, more perimeter to seal and more distance between the dehumidifier and the far corners. We handle this by sizing equipment to the actual volume and layout, adding air movement or ducting where it helps and paying attention to sections that are cut off by interior piers or walls.

It also means a larger investment, which is why we explain exactly what we are installing and why. You will see the scope in writing before anything starts.

The process in an Old Windermere home

  1. Inspection. We check moisture, framing, ducts and access, and photograph the findings.
  2. Plan. A written scope and price.
  3. Cleanout, treatment and repairs. Debris removed, mold treated, framing repaired.
  4. Drainage. Where needed.
  5. Sealing. Liner, vents and penetrations are closed.
  6. Conditioning. Insulation and dehumidifier complete the system.
  7. Walkthrough. We review the result.

A closer look at Old Windermere

Old Windermere is quiet in the way that only a mature neighborhood can be. The lots are big, the canopy is full and the houses have settled into the landscape. Many of the original owners or their families are still there, and the streets show it: long-kept gardens, well-used porches and homes that have been updated gradually rather than all at once.

That gradual updating is why crawl spaces here often hold several generations of work. A layer of original fiberglass, a second layer added in the eighties, new ductwork from a later HVAC replacement and a thin plastic sheet from a well-meaning contractor can all be found in the same space. Sorting through it takes time, and it is why we start every job with a full cleanout rather than trying to build on what is there.

Larger houses with bigger footprints also mean more systems under the floor. Water heaters, air handlers and plumbing manifolds may sit in the crawl space, and the plan needs to account for combustion air, access and service clearance around them.

Keeping an Old Windermere crawl space healthy

Large lots mean large trees, and large trees mean seasonal debris. Keep leaves and branches away from the foundation. Clear gutters before fall. Make sure downspouts discharge well away from the house. Check the dehumidifier and any sump pump at the change of seasons. And when roots approach the foundation, call before they cause damage. A short annual look at the crawl space, ideally with a flashlight and a camera, is the best way to catch problems early.

Planning for a long, careful job

Old Windermere homes are often larger than the average West Ashley cottage, and the work reflects that. A thorough cleanout of an extensive crawl space may take a day or more by itself. Sealing a large perimeter and wrapping many piers takes time, and so does sizing and placing the dehumidifier so air reaches every corner. We schedule accordingly and give you a realistic timeline in the written scope. We would rather tell you it is a four-day job and finish on time than promise two days and rush.

Local rules and practical notes

Flood zones. Some Old Windermere properties are in mapped flood zones. Confirm engineered flood vent requirements before altering them.

HOA and covenants. Most work is below the floor and does not change the exterior. If your neighborhood has rules about exterior work, check first.

Trees and landscaping. We protect landscaping and trees where possible and work around roots and shrubs near access points.

Frequently asked questions

My ranch has crawl space under part of the house and slab under the rest. Does it matter?

It affects the plan. We seal the crawl space section and address the transition to the slab so air does not move between them.

Do trees cause crawl space problems?

They contribute. Shade keeps soil damp, and roots and debris create their own issues. A liner and dehumidifier compensate.

Can you handle a large crawl space in one visit?

Larger spaces often take several days. We give you a timeline with the written scope.

Will I notice a difference in the house?

Many homeowners notice less odor, fewer humidity swings and more comfortable floors.

Do you work with other contractors?

Yes. We coordinate with HVAC, plumbing and pest control as needed.
